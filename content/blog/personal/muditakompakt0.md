<!-- Intro: I have a phone addiction -->

I have a severe addiction to my phone. I was spending close to 3-4 hours per day just doomscrolling on my phone, mostly on Instagram and YouTube. I would not say this severely interfered with my life, but it does take up valuable time and I noticed it negatively affects my mood and my sleep. More worringly was that when I tried to stop, I couldn't. I would get anxious, restless, and sooner r later I would find myself back on the phone without fully knowing when exactly I had made the conscious decision to use my phone.

<!-- Thus I was looking for a solution --> 
<!-- Two solutions considered: dumb phoens are too dumb, and smart phones are too smart -->

This prompted me to look for a solution. My first instinct was behavioral (uninstall Youtube and Instagram), but I ended up using the phones' browser to access them anyway. My next step was to use a variety of apps designed to limit my smartphone usage. This included apps and settings to set daily caps on app usage, as well as launchers that changed the layout of the screen in order to make it less colorful and the apps less accessible (e.g., OLauncher and minimalist phone).

However, these did not work, for a very simple reason: these obstacles could be overriden, and override them I did. Thus, it maybe took me three extra gestures to get to Youtube, but I would do it. Or if I hit my daily quota, I would dismiss the warning, increase the time, and keep doomscrolling.

My second approach was to search for a dumbphone. 

The problem with a dumbphone is that as much as I hate smartphones, they are useful and necessary for life. Most of my online communications happen through Whatsapp and Signal. I still need to use Google Maps (or a map equivalent) to move around. I use an online bank that requires you to use an app. And some online services require. Getting a dumbphone would require me to overhaul my life in a way that I was not prepared to do.

Thus, I was stuck. I needed something that both allowed me to run a good deal of android apps (most importantly: Whatsapp, Signal, Google Maps or an equivalent, and a banking app), as well as a device that physically prevented me from doomscrolling.


<!-- Then I came across Mudita Kompakt. -->

# Enter: The [Mudita Kompakt](https://www.mudita.com/products/phones/mudita-kompakt/)

<!-- Explain Mudita Kompakt. -->

I stumbled upon the Mudita Kompakt by chance at the IFA Berlin Expo, and immediately fell in love with it. It boasts of the following features:

* It has an e-ink display. This meant that it was literally impossible to scroll through instagram and youtube. Ok, you _technically_ can, but it is such a painful experience that I wouldn't do it.
* It runs a custom version of Android called Mudita OS that allows you sideload apps. The person at the Mudita booth showed me her phone where she had Signal and Whatsapp installed. It has its own custom Maps app, and even Uber.

This seemed like the perfect fit. It also boasted other advantages:
* 6 day battery life
* Didn't run google services, so more privacy.
* It's commitment to privacy was so strong they have a dedicated offline switch that physically disconnects all sensors (e.g., camera, microphone) so that they do not work.

<!-- Explain Journey -- plan 2 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 month update, and beyond -->
I was still worried about purchasing it. There might be some apps that might not load, and it would be a big change. Thankfully, I was in a very priviledged position in that my employer provides me with a work phone. Thus, I felt confident trying out the Mudita Kompakt, knowing I had my work phone to fall back on in case there was an essential app I needed to use or a specific instance where I needed a modern up-to-date smartphone. 

<!-- Will report back findings! -->
The more I talked to people in my life about it, the more curious and excited people were and wanted to learn about my experience. Thus, that is what I want this blog series to be. I will daily drive the mudita kompakt and document my experiences: what works, what doesn't, how I use it, and so on. There is a big push towards digital detoxing, degoogling, and more. While some people successfully manage to use dumbphones, the move might be intimating or impractical for many (I know it was for me!) The Mudita Kompakt represents potential sweet spot where it balances the parts of a smartphone you actually need and are useful whilst not letting you get to the addictive, dopamine-producing parts.
